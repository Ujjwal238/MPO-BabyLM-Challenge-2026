\section{Training Hyperparameters}
\label{sec:app-hparams}

Table~\ref{tab:hparams} lists both phases end to end. The pretraining
configuration stays deliberately close to the released GPT-BERT recipe,
and the \MPO{} phase adds no hyperparameter search beyond the ablations of
\S\ref{sec:results-ablations}. Every run in the paper, training,
refinement, and evaluation alike, executed on the same laptop.

Two pretraining choices deserve comment. The 128-token sequence length is
a hardware accommodation: the backbone's relative-position attention
leaves the fast Apple-silicon (MPS) execution path above 128 tokens, and
the corpus, dominated by transcribed dialogue, loses little at this
length. The effective batch of 128 sequences is reached through gradient
accumulation ($32 \times 4$), and the masking rate anneals from $0.30$ to
$0.15$, so early epochs face a harder reconstruction problem than late
ones.

The \MPO{} phase runs at a learning rate fifty times below the
pretraining peak, under a tighter gradient clip ($1.0$ against $2.0$).
Both choices serve the preservation principle of \S\ref{sec:method}: the
phase is meant to reorder likelihoods around an already competent
solution, not to travel far from it. Checkpoints every 250 steps yield
the trajectory of Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}b, from which step 1{,}250 is
the operating point; the full phase costs \rDpoMinutes{} minutes against
12.5 hours of pretraining.

\section{Corruption Operators and Per-Operator Ablation}
\label{sec:app-ops}

The four corruption operators behind \MPO{}'s preference pairs act on the
tokenized sentence, preserve its length, and never touch the
start-of-sequence position or padding. The adjacent swap exchanges a
random pair of neighboring tokens. The distant swap exchanges two
positions drawn independently from the whole sentence. The function-word
substitution replaces one closed-class token with a different one drawn
from the corpus's 120 most frequent non-special tokens, which in English
are overwhelmingly determiners, prepositions, auxiliaries, and pronouns.
The random replacement substitutes a uniformly drawn vocabulary token
(\S\ref{sec:method} gives worked examples of all four). \MPO{} samples
the four uniformly; the hard-negative ablation reweights toward swaps and
closed-class substitutions and away from the trivially detectable random
replacement.

Table~\ref{tab:ops} reruns the identical phase with each operator alone
(70M init, $\beta=\rBeta$, seed 42, step-1250 canary). Three regularities
emerge. First, aggregate transfer tracks the operator's proximity to the
evaluation's own contrast type: the closed-class substitution, the edit
nearest BLiMP's morphosyntactic minimal pairs, is the strongest single
choice; the swaps sit near the mixture; and the random replacement, the
edit \MPO{} already treats as trivially detectable, transfers worst.
Second, the filler--gap repair follows a different ordering: the
order-perturbing swaps repair \emph{wh\_vs\_that\_with\_gap} most
strongly ($79.0$ against the baseline's $71.5$), the substitution repairs
it partially, and the random replacement not at all. The operators are
complementary rather than redundant, as \S\ref{sec:method} intends.
Third, every variant reaches the same preference accuracy
($0.85$--$0.86$): the objective is equally learnable from any operator,
and the differences lie entirely in transfer, the proxy--evaluation
dissociation of \S\ref{sec:results} in a third form. No single operator
dominates: the substitution wins the aggregate, the swaps win the cluster
repair, and only the mixture holds the supplement's best value. The
submitted model keeps the untuned uniform mixture.

\section{A Mechanistic Reading}
\label{sec:app-mech}

A mechanistic reading consistent with the outcomes of \S\ref{sec:search}
and \S\ref{sec:results-mpo} is available. By 80M words the late
checkpoints share a basin (the checkpoint-averaging result of
Table~\ref{tab:levers}), so the saturated model occupies a region of
near-equivalent solutions; a small-learning-rate phase held to a frozen
reference moves within that region, reordering likelihoods without
leaving it, the same role anchoring plays in continual learning
\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc}. An unanchored signal injected during
acquisition instead competes with the language-modeling gradient for the
same limited capacity, and what it sacrifices first is whatever that
gradient holds most tenuously, the long-distance cluster. We offer this
as an organizing account rather than a demonstrated mechanism; the
observations it organizes are Tables~\ref{tab:levers}
and~\ref{tab:ablations}.
